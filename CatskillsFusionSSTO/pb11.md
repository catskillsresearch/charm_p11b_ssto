# Proton-Boron Fusion Reaction Sequence

The proton-boron fusion reaction is a sequential, multi-step sequence that transitions through short-lived intermediate states rather than happening all at once. The mechanism involves forming an excited **carbon-12 ($^{12}\text{C}^*$)** nucleus, which breaks down into **beryllium-8 ($^{8}\text{Be}^*$)** before finally yielding three **helium-4 ($^{4}\text{He}$)** alpha particles.

## The Real Multi-Step Sequence

* **Step 1: Proton Capture**
  * A proton fuses with boron-11.
  * This creates excited carbon-12.
  * $^{1}\text{H} + ^{11}\text{B} \rightarrow ^{12}\text{C}^*$

* **Step 2: Primary Alpha Emission**
  * Carbon-12 instantly decays.
  * It emits one alpha particle.
  * This leaves behind beryllium-8.
  * $^{12}\text{C}^* \rightarrow ^{4}\text{He} + ^{8}\text{Be}^*$

* **Step 3: Beryllium Splitting**
  * Beryllium-8 is highly unstable.
  * It splits almost immediately.
  * It yields two alpha particles.
  * $^{8}\text{Be}^* \rightarrow 2\ ^{4}\text{He}$

## The Complete Pathway

The true sequential reaction pathway is written as:

$$^{1}\text{H} + ^{11}\text{B} \rightarrow ^{12}\text{C}^* \rightarrow ^{4}\text{He} + ^{8}\text{Be}^* \rightarrow 3\ ^{4}\text{He}$$

This step-by-step nature explains why aneutronic fusion reactor designs produce safely contained charged particles instead of harmful, high-energy neutrons.
